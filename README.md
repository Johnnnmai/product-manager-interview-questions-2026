# Product Manager Interview Questions 2026: 10 Free Practice Questions

Ten practice questions for product manager interviews, picked from the 100-question Valenx Press Product Manager Interview Question Bank. They cover the five areas PM interview loops tend to test: product sense, metrics, execution and prioritization, strategy, and behavioral and leadership. They are a mix of Entry, Mid and Senior levels.

Each question comes with a short hint, not a full answer. Try your own answer first, then use the hint to see if you covered the key idea.

## How to practice
1. **Set a timer.** Give yourself 3-5 minutes per question and answer out loud or in writing, as if you were in the interview.
2. **Answer before you read the hint.** The hint names what a strong answer has to include. It is not the full answer.
3. **Score yourself from 1 to 5.** 1 = can't define it; 3 = can explain it with one example; 5 = can draw it, compare alternatives and discuss edge cases.
4. **Redo anything you scored 3 or lower** two days later, without notes.
5. **Check one written answer for free** with the [answer-check tool](https://johnnnmai.github.io/ai-engineer-interview-questions/answer-check/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=pm_sample).

For the behavioral questions, use a real example from your own work. A borrowed or made-up story falls apart at the first follow-up question.

### Product sense interview questions

**1. [Entry] How do you distinguish a user problem from a feature request?**
*Hint:* Name the user and their pain point before any solution. A request for a feature is not yet proof of the need behind it. Tie success to the problem, not to shipping the feature.

**2. [Mid] How would you define the boundaries of an AI assistant feature?**
*Hint:* Start from the user problem. Then say what the assistant will and will not do, where a person must confirm an action, and which alternatives you rejected and why.

### Product metrics interview questions

**3. [Entry] How do you choose a metric for a product goal?**
*Hint:* One goal first, then one named metric that tracks it. Say what a change in that metric would tell you and what you would do about it.

**4. [Senior] Adoption is rising, but user errors are not going down. What do you do?**
*Hint:* Treat adoption and error rate as separate evidence. Look at bad cases and user feedback before deciding the next iteration, and don't let usage stand in for "the problem is solved".

### Execution and prioritization interview questions

**5. [Mid] A new feature request arrives mid-project. What do you do?**
*Hint:* Restate the agreed scope and the fixed constraints. Show what existing work would move out if the new request moves in, and record the decision.

**6. [Senior] How should you escalate a launch-blocking risk?**
*Hint:* Describe the blocker and its impact on delivery, what was already tried at the working level, and the specific decision you need from the person who can unblock it.

### Product strategy interview questions

**7. [Mid] How do you make a strategy recommendation when time and headcount are fixed?**
*Hint:* Write down the constraints first. Lay out more than one way to do the work, rank them by must-have value against effort and risk, then commit and control later additions.

**8. [Senior] How do you handle market size in a strategy answer when you have no reliable market data?**
*Hint:* Say that you have no data. State each assumption as an assumption, name the input that would replace it, and move on to the user, the product's value and how it wins. Never present a guess as evidence.

### Behavioral PM interview questions

**9. [Entry] Tell me about a time you influenced someone without authority.**
*Hint:* Show whose priorities differed, what you personally did, how the collaboration changed, and what you learned. Claim only the outcome that actually happened.

**10. [Senior] Tell me about a decision you would reverse if you could repeat a project.**
*Hint:* Give the original context, your action and the real result. Name the assumption or tradeoff you would handle differently, and don't rewrite the story into a success.
